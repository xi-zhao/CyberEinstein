Exact zero-capacity boundary for strongly dephased bosonic loss channels

Research derivation and reproducible certificate, 13 October 2026.

Status. This note contains a proposed new infinite-dimensional theorem, a complete constructive proof, an exact rational certificate for its stated parameter range, and independent finite-channel implementation checks. It is an internally reviewed research result, not an externally peer-reviewed or proof-assistant-certified publication. It proves the strong-dephasing portion of Conjecture 40 of [R1]; it does not establish the conjectured largest parameter range. Literature comparison and its limitations are recorded separately in NOVELTY_AUDIT.md.

1. Model, operational question, and theorem

Use the convention

\[ \mathcal N_{\eta,\gamma}=\mathcal E_\eta\circ\mathcal D_\gamma, \qquad \mathcal D_\gamma(|n\rangle\langle m|) =e^{-\gamma(n-m)^2/2}|n\rangle\langle m|, \qquad q=e^{-\gamma}. \]

Here \(\mathcal E_\eta\) is the bosonic pure-loss channel with vacuum environment and transmissivity \(\eta\). Its Fock action is

\[ \mathcal N_{\eta,\gamma}(|n\rangle\langle m|) =q^{(n-m)^2/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min(n,m)} \sqrt{\binom n\ell\binom m\ell} (1-\eta)^\ell\eta^{(n+m)/2-\ell} |n-\ell\rangle\langle m-\ell|. \tag{1} \]

The question is the sign of the unassisted quantum capacity of independent uses of this channel, allowing arbitrary collective encoding and decoding. For an energy constraint, it is the usual mean input photon-number constraint per use, not an imposed minimum photon number. No preshared entanglement, two-way classical feedback, postselection, access to the environment, or intermediate error correction inside the noise interval is assumed.

Theorem. For every \(0<q\le1/20\), and every \(0\le\eta\le1\),

\[ \mathcal N_{\eta,-\ln q}\text{ is anti-degradable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta\le\eta_c(q):=\frac1{1+q}. \tag{2} \]

Moreover, for every positive mean energy allowance \(E>0\),

\[ Q_E(\mathcal N_{\eta,-\ln q})=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta\le\frac1{1+q}, \tag{3} \]

and \(Q_E>0\) above that line. This identifies the boundary, not the value of the positive capacity. The same sign statement applies without an energy constraint.

The anti-degradable direction is the new constructive part. The necessity already follows from the qubit restriction in [R1, Theorems 2/38]. Section 6 gives a separate coherent-information argument that proves strict positivity above the line; non-anti-degradability alone would not suffice.

2. A binomial Gram decomposition suffices

Set

\[ G_{nm}=q^{(n-m)^2/2},\qquad B_{nk}=\binom nk(1-q)^kq^{n-k}\quad(0\le k\le n), \]

and \(B_{nk}=0\) otherwise. The kernel \(G\) is positive semidefinite: it is the Gram matrix of real coherent states \(|\sqrt\gamma n\rangle\), or equivalently of the Gaussian phase functions.

We will construct kernels \(A^{(k)}\succeq0\) satisfying

\[ A^{(k)}_{nn}=B_{nk},\qquad \sum_{k=0}^\infty A^{(k)}_{nm}=G_{nm}. \tag{4} \]

Each matrix element of the sum is finite because positivity and the zero diagonal force \(A^{(k)}_{nm}=0\) when \(k>\min(n,m)\). For \(n,m\ge k\) define the correlation kernel

\[ K^{(k)}_{nm}=\frac{A^{(k)}_{nm}}{\sqrt{B_{nk}B_{mk}}}. \tag{5} \]

It is positive semidefinite and has unit diagonal. Thus there exist unit vectors \(|e_n^{(k)}\rangle\) with this Gram kernel. Countably many such vectors can be realized in separable Hilbert spaces.

2.1 An explicit two-output isometry

At \(\eta=1/(1+q)\), set

\[ r=1-\eta=\frac q{1+q},\qquad s=2\eta-1=\frac{1-q}{1+q},\qquad 2r+s=1. \]

Define on every Fock basis vector

\[ V|n\rangle= \sum_{k=0}^n\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k} \sqrt{\frac{n!}{\ell!\,(n-k-\ell)!\,k!}} \,r^{(n-k)/2}s^{k/2} |n-\ell\rangle_{B_1}|\ell+k\rangle_{B_2} |e_{n-\ell}^{(k)}\rangle_{E_1} |e_{\ell+k}^{(k)}\rangle_{E_2}|k\rangle_{E_3}. \tag{6} \]

Every sum in (6) is finite. Distinct terms for a fixed \(n\) have orthogonal output/register labels, and their squared coefficients sum to \((2r+s)^n=1\). For different \(n,m\), terms with distinct \(k\) are orthogonal; at equal \(k\), the total output photon number is \(n+k\), respectively \(m+k\). Hence \(V^\dagger V=I\). It extends by continuity to an isometry on the full bosonic Hilbert space. This is the infinite-dimensional step; no limit of numerical feasibility tests is being used.

When \(B_2,E_1,E_2,E_3\) are traced out, surviving terms have the same \(k\), and \(\ell\) on bra and ket. Their coefficient is the ordinary loss coefficient in (1), times

\[ \sum_k\sqrt{B_{n-\ell,k}B_{m-\ell,k}} K^{(k)}_{n-\ell,m-\ell} =G_{n-\ell,m-\ell}=G_{nm}. \tag{7} \]

The last equality uses the translation-invariance of the Gaussian dephasing kernel. Thus the \(B_1\) marginal is exactly \(\mathcal N\). By the exchange symmetry of (6), the \(B_2\) marginal is identical. Taking the complement of \(B_1\) to be \(B_2E_1E_2E_3\), tracing out \(E_1E_2E_3\) is an anti-degrading map. This proves anti-degradability directly, without an infinite-dimensional maximally entangled Choi vector.

The isometry is a mathematical channel dilation, not a proposed passive optical implementation. Its ability to create a second noisy output is allowed in a converse proof; it does not copy an unknown pure input.

It remains to establish (4).

3. Remove the two saturated low-photon directions

Define two vectors, indexed by \(n\ge0\),

\[ u_{0n}=q^{n^2/2},\qquad u_{1n}=q^{(n-1)^2/2}\frac{1-q^n}{\sqrt{1-q}},\quad u_{10}=0. \tag{8} \]

These are the first two Gram-Schmidt columns of \(G\). Consequently

\[ R=G-u_0u_0^\mathsf T-u_1u_1^\mathsf T\succeq0, \tag{9} \]

and rows/columns \(0,1\) vanish. This assertion can equivalently be verified by the Schur complement of the principal block \(\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt q\\\sqrt q&1\end{pmatrix}\).

Define nonnegative residual probabilities

\[ p_{n0}=B_{n0}-u_{0n}^2, \quad p_{n1}=B_{n1}-u_{1n}^2, \quad p_{nk}=B_{nk}\ (k\ge2). \tag{10} \]

For \(n=0,1\), all \(p_{nk}=0\). For \(n\ge2\), \(p_{n0}=q^n-q^{n^2}\ge0\). For the other potentially nontrivial column,

\[ \frac{u_{1n}^2}{B_{n1}} =\frac{q^{(n-1)(n-2)}}n \left(\frac{1-q^n}{1-q}\right)^2. \]

At \(n=2\) this is \((1+q)^2/2<1\). For \(n\ge3\) it is at most \(q^2/[3(1-q)^2]<1\). Both hold throughout \(q\le1/20\). Also \(\sum_kp_{nk}=R_{nn}>0\) for \(n\ge2\).

For \(n,m\ge2\), set

\[ H_{nm}=\sum_k\sqrt{p_{nk}p_{mk}},\qquad C_{nm}=R_{nm}/H_{nm}. \tag{11} \]

The denominator is positive (e.g. the \(k=2\) term). The diagonal of \(C\) is one. Moreover,

\[ \frac{R_{nm}}{G_{nm}} =1-q^{nm} -\frac{q^{(n-1)(m-1)}(1-q^n)(1-q^m)}{1-q}, \tag{12} \]

so \(0\le R_{nm}\le G_{nm}\). Indeed the right side is at least \(1-q^4-q/(1-q)>0\) in the range under consideration.

If \(C\succeq0\), define \(D_k=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{p_{nk}})\), with zeros in its first two rows, and

\[ A^{(0)}=u_0u_0^\mathsf T+D_0CD_0, \quad A^{(1)}=u_1u_1^\mathsf T+D_1CD_1, \quad A^{(k)}=D_kCD_k\ (k\ge2). \tag{13} \]

They are positive, have the prescribed binomial diagonal, and sum entrywise to \(u_0u_0^\mathsf T+u_1u_1^\mathsf T+C\circ H=G\). Thus (4) follows from positivity of the residual kernel \(C\).

4. Uniform positivity of the residual kernel

4.1 A bound valid at arbitrarily high photon number

For \(m\ge2,d\ge1\), define

\[ z_m(q)=q^m+m(1-q)q^{m-1}, \]

\[ U_{m,d}(q)= \frac{q^{d(d-1)/2}}{1-z_m(q)} \sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^d\frac{mq+j}{m+j}}. \tag{14} \]

We claim \(0\le C_{m,m+d}\le U_{m,d}(q)\).

Because \(p_{nk}=B_{nk}\) for \(k\ge2\),

\[ H_{m,m+d}\ge\sum_{k=2}^{m}\sqrt{B_{mk}B_{m+d,k}}. \]

Let \(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,q)\) be the number of failures. Rewriting the last sum gives

\[ q^{d/2}\sqrt{(m+1)_d} \mathbb E\left[f_d(L)\,1_{L\le m-2}\right], \quad f_d(x)=\prod_{j=1}^d(x+j)^{-1/2}. \]

The function \(f_d\) is decreasing and convex: \((\log f_d)''>0\), and \(f_d''=f_d[(\log f_d)'^2+(\log f_d)'']>0\). Jensen yields \(\mathbb E f_d(L)\ge f_d(mq)\). Since \(mq\le m-1\) in the stated range, the omitted terms obey

\[ \mathbb E[f_d(L)1_{L\ge m-1}]\le z_m(q)f_d(mq). \]

It follows that

\[ H_{m,m+d}\ge q^{d/2}\sqrt{\frac{(m+1)_d}{(mq+1)_d}}[1-z_m(q)]. \]

Combining with \(R_{m,m+d}\le q^{d^2/2}\) proves (14).

For fixed \(m,d\), \(U_{m,d}(q)\) increases with \(q\). For fixed \(q,d\), it decreases with \(m\). To see the latter, \(z_m\) decreases with \(m\), while each factor \((mq+j)/(m+j)=q+(1-q)j/(m+j)\) also decreases. Explicitly,

\[ z_m-z_{m+1}=m(1-q)^2q^{m-1}\ge0,\qquad \partial_q z_m=m(m-1)(1-q)q^{m-2}\ge0. \]

Therefore it suffices to bound the kernel at \(q_0=1/20\).

For \(d\ge2\),

\[ U_{m,d}(q)\le\frac{q^{d(d-1)/2}}{(1-q)^2}, \quad \sum_{d\ge D}U_{m,d}(q) \le\frac{q^{D(D-1)/2}}{(1-q)^2(1-q^D)},\quad D\ge2. \tag{15} \]

Here \(z_m\le z_2=2q-q^2\), and consecutive exponents in the series differ by at least \(D\).

4.2 A fully hand-readable subrange, q <= 1/100

This simpler variant establishes the existential strong-dephasing result without the optimized seven-weight certificate. Put \(w_2=3/4\), \(w_n=1\) for \(n\ge3\), and let \(a_m=U_{m,1}\). At \(q\le1/100\), direct squaring of rational inequalities gives

\[ a_2\le0.60,\quad a_3\le0.51,\quad a_4\le0.46, \quad \sum_{d\ge2}U_{m,d}\le0.011. \]

The weighted off-diagonal row sums, divided by \(w_n\), are bounded by

\[ \frac43(0.60+0.011)<1\quad(n=2), \] \[ \frac34(0.60)+0.51+2(0.011)=0.982<1\quad(n=3), \] \[ 0.51+0.46+2(0.011)=0.992<1\quad(n\ge4). \]

The numbers here are terminating rationals and the inequalities can be checked without a floating-point eigensolver.

src/verify_infinite_kernel.py checks the seven rows and (16) using only Python integers and fractions.Fraction. For each positive rational radicand \(v\), it selects the smallest integer \(s\) satisfying \(s^2\ge 10^{28}v\) and uses \(s/10^{14}\) as a rational square-root upper bound. It checks this squared inequality exactly. The full rational numerators/denominators are in results/infinite_kernel_certificate.json. Neither a numerical LP solution nor a truncated eigenvalue is the certificate. The LP was used only to discover weights, which were then rounded and independently checked.

For every finite principal submatrix, \(\operatorname{diag}(w)C\operatorname{diag}(w)\) has nonnegative diagonal and is diagonally dominant in absolute row sum. It is therefore positive semidefinite. All finite principal submatrices are positive, which is exactly positivity as a countable Gram kernel. In fact the certificate gives a strictly positive relative margin greater than 0.00228. This proves the required \(C\succeq0\) for all photon indices and all \(0<q\le1/20\), and hence the construction (13), isometry (6), and anti-degradability at the boundary.

5. Complete the anti-degradability region and compare the old construction

For \(\eta<\eta_c\), apply an additional pure-loss channel \(\mathcal E_{\eta/\eta_c}\) to each broadcast output. Each marginal becomes the desired \(\mathcal N_{\eta,\gamma}\). Hence every channel below the boundary is anti-degradable. The converse above the boundary follows either from [R1, Theorem 38] or from the strict capacity positivity in Section 6.

The Hadamard kernel of [R1, Theorem 41] at this boundary is

\[ \widetilde C_{nm}=G_{nm}/\sum_k\sqrt{B_{nk}B_{mk}}. \]

Its first three indices give

\[ \widetilde C_{01}=1,\quad \widetilde C_{02}=q,\quad \widetilde C_{12}=\frac1{q+\sqrt2(1-q)}. \]

Consequently the determinant of that 3-by-3 principal block is

\[ -\left(q-\frac1{q+\sqrt2(1-q)}\right)^2<0\quad(0<q<1). \tag{17} \]

Thus simply proving positivity of the old Hadamard kernel more carefully cannot reach the exact boundary. Our construction changes the channel extension: the dephasing correlation kernel is conditioned on the binomial outcome \(k\), and the saturated first two Gram directions are removed before applying a common residual kernel. This distinguishes the mechanism from merely refining the diagonal-dominance estimate in [R1]. It does not prove global literature priority.

6. Strictly positive capacity above the boundary

Consider \(\rho_x=(1-x)|0\rangle\langle0|+x|1\rangle\langle1|\), \(0<x<1\). Its mean energy is \(x\). The output entropy is \(h_2(\eta x)\). For the purification \(\sqrt{1-x}|00\rangle+\sqrt x|11\rangle\), the joint reference-output state’s nonzero eigenvalues are \((1-\eta)x\) and \(\lambda_\pm\), where

\[ \lambda_++\lambda_-=1-(1-\eta)x, \quad \lambda_+\lambda_-=\eta(1-q)x(1-x). \]

Expanding at small positive \(x\),

\[ I_c(\rho_x,\mathcal N) =[\eta(1+q)-1]x\log_2(1/x)+O(x). \tag{18} \]

For every fixed \(q>0\) and \(\eta>1/(1+q)\), the leading coefficient is positive. A sufficiently small \(x\) therefore has positive coherent information. Standard finite-dimensional quantum coding applied to this invariant two-level input/output restriction gives a positive achievable quantum rate. For any \(E>0\), choose \(x<E\) with a strict energy margin; the same coding argument respects the usual asymptotic average-energy constraint.

This argument does not suppose that every non-anti-degradable channel has positive capacity. It constructs a witness in this specific family. It also does not promise a useful numerical throughput near the threshold: the required \(x\), and the lower-bound rate, can be extremely small. For example, at \(q=0.05,\eta=0.953\), a verified input \(x=10^{-128}\) gives only about \(6.31\times10^{-131}\) qubits/use. The result is a sign theorem.

Because the broadcast construction establishes anti-degradability below and on the boundary, the quantum capacity there vanishes. This completes (3). The model’s two-way assisted capacity is positive for \(\eta>0\) and finite \(\gamma\), as already proved in [R1, Theorem 42]; no contradiction is involved.

7. Exact finite-time disappearance of unassisted quantum capacity

For the memoryless Lindblad evolution

\[ \dot\rho=\kappa\mathcal D[a](\rho)+\kappa_\phi\mathcal D[n](\rho), \quad n=a^\dagger a, \quad\mathcal D[L](\rho)=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}, \]

with \(\kappa,\kappa_\phi>0\), the finite-time map has

\[ \eta(t)=e^{-\kappa t},\qquad q(t)=e^{-\kappa_\phi t}. \]

The two generators commute on the Fock matrix units, since photon loss preserves the bra-ket number difference. Let \(t_*\) be the unique positive solution

\[ e^{-\kappa t_*}(1+e^{-\kappa_\phi t_*})=1. \tag{19} \]

If

\[ \frac{\kappa_\phi}{\kappa} \ge\frac{\ln20}{\ln(21/20)} =61.4003319019\ldots, \tag{20} \]

then \(q(t_*)\le1/20\). For every \(t<t_*\), (18) proves positive capacity, regardless of whether the strong-dephasing condition has yet been reached. For every \(t\ge t_*\), the channel lies in the anti-degradable region proved above. Thus \(t_*\) is the exact first zero-capacity time for this noise model and operational setting.

For example, at \(\kappa_\phi/\kappa=100\),

\[ \kappa t_*=0.03372581431726\ldots,\qquad \eta(t_*)=0.9668365610411\ldots . \]

Only about 3.316% of the initial mean photon number has been lost when unassisted quantum capacity reaches zero. The possibility that strong dephasing combines with arbitrarily weak loss to kill capacity was already established in [R1]; the increment here is the exact boundary/time in the stated regime, not the qualitative effect. The result is not a bound on a continuously actively corrected memory, on two-way assisted storage, or on a device without the stated noise law.

8. Verification, failed branches, and limitations

The acceptance argument is analytic plus exact rational inequalities. It is independent of the exploratory semidefinite solver. verify_broadcast.py builds (13) with 90/110-digit arithmetic and checks both full two-output marginals against a separately built loss-Kraus/dephasing Choi channel. Finite tests go through nine Fock levels for the full joint channel and twenty-five levels for coefficient identities; these sizes are implementation checks, not cutoffs in the theorem.

capacity_consequences.py independently exponentiates the Lindblad superoperator and compares it with the finite-time channel. It also evaluates high-precision coherent-information witnesses and scalar roots of (19). verify_infinite_kernel.py has no numerical-library dependency.

Exploratory SDP timeouts and inaccurate statuses are retained as unresolved numerical attempts, never as proof of infeasibility. A Gram-Schmidt measurement followed by a classical stochastic correction produced negative coefficients and was abandoned. The shared residual-kernel construction itself has negative finite principal eigenvalues around \(q=0.4\); this rejects extrapolation of this particular construction, not the underlying conjecture. The seven-weight certificate establishes only \(q\le1/20\). A stronger method may extend it.

No test demonstrates that a cognitive-map loop outperforms an equal-budget research baseline. The online research and proof decisions were made by the current model using tools; the saved scripts verify the result and preserve failed branches, rather than autonomously rediscovering the topic from scratch.
