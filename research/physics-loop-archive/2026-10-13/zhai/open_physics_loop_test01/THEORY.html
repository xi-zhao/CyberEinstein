State-level obstruction to an entanglement-LSM proof route

Research note, 13 October 2026. Internal derivation, not external peer review.

1. Exact question and scope

Zhou, Li, Zhai, Li and Gu [R1] propose an entanglement-spectrum LSM constraint and discuss a proof route from short correlations to approximate spatial Markov structure, then to a sufficiently local modular Hamiltonian, then to a vanishing twist cost. The published main text still labels the approximate-Markov-to-locality step a conjecture. It also motivates local system–bath coupling and local gapped-ladder examples.

We test ONLY the implication from symmetry, uniform decay of state correlations, and small conditional mutual information to a low spectral gap of K=-log rho. We do not claim a counterexample within a fixed, uniformly local, uniformly gapped parent-Hamiltonian or local-Lindbladian preparation class. The paper's numerical ladder results are not reproduced or refuted.

“Markov” here refers to spatial conditional mutual information of a state, not to the temporal Markov approximation of a dynamical bath.

2. A full-rank translation- and SO(3)-invariant family

Let L>=4 be even, D=2^L, and let T translate a periodic spin-1/2 chain by one site. Define a nearest-neighbor dimer product

\[ |v_L\rangle=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L/2} (|\uparrow_{2j-1}\downarrow_{2j}\rangle-|\downarrow_{2j-1}\uparrow_{2j}\rangle)/\sqrt2. \]

Since T^2|v_L>=|v_L>, the nonzero normalized vector

\[ |\psi_L\rangle=\frac{|v_L\rangle+T|v_L\rangle} {\|\,|v_L\rangle+T|v_L\rangle\|} \]

is a total-spin singlet with translation eigenvalue +1. Set P_L=|psi_L><psi_L|. For fixed alpha>0, independent of L, define

\[ \rho_L=\frac{I+\alpha P_L}{D+\alpha} =(1-p_L)\tau_L+p_LP_L, \quad \tau_L=I/D,\quad p_L=\frac{\alpha}{D+\alpha}. \tag{1} \]

This is full rank and invariant under one-site translation and global spin rotations. These are weak symmetries (U rho U†=rho), not support in one fixed total-spin sector.

Its largest eigenvalue is (1+alpha)/(D+alpha), unique; the other D-1 eigenvalues equal 1/(D+alpha). In particular its condition number is the constant 1+alpha, not a diverging one.

3. Uniform clustering

For disjoint observables A,B with operator norm at most one, tau is a product state. The change of the AB expectation from tau is at most 2p_L; the change of the product of one-point expectations is at most 4p_L. Consequently

\[ |\langle AB\rangle_{\rho_L}-\langle A\rangle_{\rho_L}\langle B\rangle_{\rho_L}| \le 6p_L\le6\alpha2^{-L}. \tag{2} \]

For the usual ring distance r<=L/2, this gives a uniform exponential-clustering bound 6alpha exp(-2r ln2), with constants independent of L. Single-site traceless spin observables have the sharper p_L bound because singlets have maximally mixed one-site marginals.

This is exponential upper-bound clustering, not a claim of a nonzero fitted correlation length or a specific local preparation mechanism. Every fixed-size reduced state tends to the maximally mixed state. The family has not been shown projectively consistent under changing L; no fixed local thermodynamic parent is asserted.

4. Conditional mutual information is exponentially small for every partition

For any disjoint A,B,C (they need not fill the chain), let R=ABC. With natural logarithms,

\[ I(A:C|B)_\rho\le I(AB:C)_\rho \le D(\rho_R\|\tau_R)\le D(\rho_L\|\tau_L). \tag{3} \]

The first inequality is the chain rule and I(B:C)>=0. The second follows by decomposing relative entropy to a product maximally mixed state into mutual information and nonnegative marginal relative entropies. The last is data processing under partial trace.

The global relative entropy is exactly

\[ s_L:=D(\rho_L\|\tau_L) =-\ln(1+\alpha/D)+\frac{1+\alpha}{D+\alpha}\ln(1+\alpha) =\frac{(1+\alpha)\ln(1+\alpha)-\alpha}{2^L}+O(4^{-L}). \tag{4} \]

In particular s_L<=(1+alpha)ln(1+alpha)2^{-L}. If a shielding buffer has length ell<=L, this is also bounded by a constant times 2^{-ell}, uniformly in L and the partition. Thus the counterexample satisfies a stronger entropy condition than checking selected buffers.

The finite calculations test examples of (3). Equation (3), not finite-size sampling, supplies the all-partition conclusion.

5. Yet the entanglement gap and the twist cost remain finite

The logarithm is elementary:

\[ K_L=-\ln\rho_L=\ln(D+\alpha)I-\ln(1+\alpha)P_L. \tag{5} \]

It has a unique ground state psi_L and gap

\[ \boxed{\Delta_K=\ln(1+\alpha)>0} \tag{6} \]

for every L. K is explicitly nonlocal. It is not a short-range Hamiltonian to which the original closed-system LSM theorem applies.

For U_tw=exp[(2pi i/L) sum_j j S_j^z], the spin-1/2 and singlet properties imply that U_tw psi has momentum pi relative to psi. They are orthogonal. Therefore

\[ \langle U_{\rm tw}\psi_L|K_L|U_{\rm tw}\psi_L\rangle- \langle\psi_L|K_L|\psi_L\rangle=\ln(1+\alpha), \tag{7} \]

not O(1/L). Translation, singlet properties, orthogonality and this cost were checked by independent dense operator calculations at L=4,6,8; vector-only symmetry checks were made at L=10,12.

Precise mathematical conclusion: the listed state-level hypotheses, even with full rank, bounded condition number and a uniform all-partition CMI bound, do not force the proposed low-spectrum conclusion. This does not settle stronger preparation-dependent hypotheses.

One further subtlety: small individual Pauli coefficients do not by themselves establish the summable interaction-norm locality required for an LSM twist bound. Exponentially many nonlocal terms can have a finite combined effect. We use the exact twist cost, not a visual inspection of coefficients, as the diagnostic.

6. What control of a nearby Gibbs state would be sufficient?

Small trace distance and relative entropy are insufficient. Here

\[ \tfrac12\|\rho_L-\tau_L\|_1 =\frac{\alpha(D-1)}{D(D+\alpha)}\longrightarrow0, \quad \inf_c\|K_L+\ln\tau_L-cI\|_\infty =\tfrac12\ln(1+\alpha). \tag{8} \]

A sufficient, substantially stronger comparison is the operator-order sandwich

\[ e^{-\epsilon_L}\sigma_L\preceq\rho_L\preceq e^{\epsilon_L}\sigma_L. \tag{9} \]

Operator monotonicity of logarithm gives

\[ \|-\ln\rho_L+\ln\sigma_L\|_\infty\le\epsilon_L. \]

Weyl's eigenvalue inequalities then give

\[ |\Delta(-\ln\rho_L)-\Delta(-\ln\sigma_L)|\le2\epsilon_L. \tag{10} \]

Thus a genuinely local, symmetric reference Gibbs state with vanishing low gap would transfer that property if epsilon_L tends to zero. This is only a sufficient repair of the implication; we have not proved it follows from local bath preparation, nor is operator-log stability claimed as a new mathematical theorem. Kato–Brandão [R2] proves relative-entropy approximation to local Gibbs states; it must not be silently strengthened to (9).

7. Consequence for attempts to certify the gap from copies of rho

The following is an elementary information-theoretic consequence, not a separate novelty claim. With equal priors, optimal discrimination of rho_L^tensor m from tau_L^tensor m has success (1+T_m)/2. Quantum Pinsker in natural logarithms gives

\[ T_m\le\sqrt{m s_L/2}. \]

Success at least 2/3 therefore requires

\[ m\ge\frac{2}{9s_L}=\Omega(2^L) \tag{11} \]

at fixed alpha. The weaker bound m>=1/(3T_1) follows directly by a telescoping trace-norm argument. These allow collective measurements on all copies. They apply to discrimination of this promised pair, not to every physical entanglement-spectrum experiment or to scenarios where a trusted local generative model is already supplied.

For alpha=1 and L=32 the Pinsker bound is at least 2,470,750,996 copies. No 2^32-dimensional matrix was stored: these values evaluate the exact expression (4) at 100-digit precision.

8. Scope, novelty and physical relevance

  1. The failure of nonlinear spectral quantities to follow from averaged state distances is a general mathematical fact. We do not present that fact, the rank-one spike idea, or Pinsker/Weyl bounds as independently original discoveries.
  2. The concrete spin-singlet construction applies that obstruction to the state-level entanglement-LSM proof route in [R1]. It is a useful precise diagnosis, not a demonstrated contradiction of the physical ladder models.
  3. [R3] already discusses nonlocal entanglement Hamiltonians in an XXZ ladder. Its sector-resolved results and broken SU(2) setting differ; our construction does not warrant ignoring it and claiming the first nonlocal EH example.
  4. The supplementary ZIP of the published article could not be accessed. A physical-parent counterclaim is not cleared by this review. General searches were noisy, so no global priority claim is made.
  5. To justify a larger next stage, one must settle whether a fixed, physically local preparation class supplies the spectral regularity absent here, or construct a counterexample satisfying that class. More finite-spin points of (1) cannot answer it and are not approved.
